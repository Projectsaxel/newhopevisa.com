What is the Temporary Protected Status (TPS) service?

TPS is a humanitarian program that allows nationals from designated countries to live and work temporarily in the U.S. when returning home is unsafe due to war, disaster, or instability.

Our immigration office in Florida assists with all forms and documentation for new TPS applications and extensions. We ensure your submission meets all USCIS standards and deadlines for approval.

This service helps you stay protected, work legally, and maintain valid immigration status while your country recovers.

How does the TPS process work?

Understanding how TPS works helps you stay compliant and prepared for each stage of your immigration process in Florida.

  1. Check eligibility and confirm your country’s TPS designation
  2. Prepare necessary documents and forms
  3. Submit TPS application (Form I-821) and optional work permit (Form I-765)
  4. Attend biometrics appointment if required
  5. Receive USCIS decision and EAD card (if applicable)
  6. Renew or re-register when new TPS periods are announced

Our immigration consultants guide you through each step, ensuring your TPS application or renewal is complete and approved without complications.

FAQ

Here are the most common questions about Temporary Protected Status (TPS) in Florida, answered clearly to help you understand the process.

Who qualifies for TPS?

Nationals of countries designated by the U.S. government who have lived continuously in the U.S. since a specific date may qualify for TPS.

Can I work with TPS?

Yes, you can apply for an Employment Authorization Document (EAD) while under TPS protection to work legally in the U.S.

How long does TPS last?

TPS designations typically last 6 to 18 months but can be extended by the U.S. government depending on conditions in your country.

Can I travel abroad with TPS?

Yes, but you must apply for advance parole before leaving the U.S. to maintain your TPS status upon return.

What happens if my TPS expires?

You must re-register during the designated period to maintain your status and work authorization. Late filings may risk denial.

Can TPS lead to a green card?

Not directly, but it can provide stability while you explore other immigration options with professional guidance.

What documents are required for TPS?

Proof of identity, nationality, continuous residence, and entry into the U.S. are typically required for TPS applications.

Do I need a lawyer to apply?

It’s not mandatory, but professional help ensures your forms, evidence, and timing meet all USCIS requirements for approval.
